## Problem Statement

115.2 million Americans have prediabetes. Most receive minimal guidance beyond "eat better."
The daily lived experience: standing in front of a meal and not knowing if it's safe to eat.
This pre-meal paralysis causes unnecessary food avoidance — not because foods are actually
dangerous [REVIEW NEEDED: Replace restriction-framing with permission-first language], but because the information available (Google results) is contradictory, slow,
and not personalized. People aren't failing to manage prediabetes; they're failing to get
clear answers fast enough.

Revora answers one question: "Can I eat this?" — in under 5 seconds, personalized to
a specific A1C level, with a clear next action.

---

## Demand Evidence

- **Personal experience**: Founder has prediabetes. Daily pre-meal anxiety is lived reality.
- **External validation**: Aunt also has prediabetes — independent confirmation of the same
  behavioral pattern (pre-meal paralysis, not post-meal regret).
- **Status quo observed**: Both people currently Google every meal. When results are
  contradictory, they default to avoidance. Result: shrinking diet, loss of food enjoyment.
- **Community signal**: r/prediabetes shows the same pattern at scale (60% of posts express
  nutritional paralysis, per PRD research).
- **Competitive validation**: Glycemic Snap (glycemicsnap.com) exists and serves a general
  audience — proving the photo + GL mechanic has paying users. Their general framing is
  the gap Revora fills with prediabetes-specific coaching.

---

## Status Quo

Users Google "does [food] spike blood sugar?" 3-5 times per day.
- Results are contradictory across sites
- No personalization to their actual A1C or dietary profile
- No action guidance — just generic "it can be high GI"
- When uncertain, default to avoidance
- Outcome: diet shrinks over time, food anxiety increases, food enjoyment decreases

The workaround costs: 10-15 minutes/day of research that produces worse outcomes than
doing nothing, because avoidance is worse than informed moderation.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Real lead users**: The founder (has prediabetes) and their aunt (has prediabetes).
Not composites — actual people with this problem today.

**Archetype**: Newly diagnosed prediabetic, 30-60 years old, recently got a scary A1C
reading from their doctor, immediately started Googling everything. Currently avoiding
whole food categories they could safely eat with guidance. Their fear: progression to
Type 2 diabetes. Their daily question: "Can I eat this?"

**Narrowest wedge**: A text-based "Should I eat this?" tool (MVP scope only).
- Input: food name (or description) + A1C level
- Output: spike risk in plain English + one sequencing tip (if MODERATE/HIGH) + one swap
- No scanner. No app. No account creation. No database. No payment.
- This is a signal tool, not the final product. Its only job: prove WTP before building.

## Constraints

- Pre-product. No code written.
- Solo founder (or small team).
- Prediabetes-specific only — not expanding to Type 2 or general health in this MVP.
- **Tone: Permission-first.** For SAFE results, the output should feel like a gift, not a
  passing grade. For MODERATE/HIGH results, conservative bias applies — when uncertain,
  treat as higher risk and recommend the swap or sequencing adjustment.
- All advice must be grounded in evidence (Shukla et al. 2019, Imai et al. 2023 for sequencing).
- Must never give medical advice — always include "consult your doctor" footer on every result.

### API Cost Estimate (MVP)

- GPT-4o text: ~$0.005–0.015 per query (input ~300 tokens + output ~200 tokens)
- A successful r/prediabetes post: estimate 200–500 queries in first 48 hours
- Total exposure: $1–8. No circuit breaker needed for this scale.
- **Gate**: If a post goes unexpectedly viral (>2,000 queries in 24h), add simple rate
  limiting: one query per IP per hour via Vercel edge middleware. Use Vercel KV for the
  counter store. Note: verify current Vercel KV free-tier limits before relying on this
  (as of 2026, the Hobby tier is request-count limited, not storage-limited; confirm limits
  at vercel.com/docs/storage/vercel-kv before launch). Fallback if KV is unavailable:
  add a Cloudflare free-tier proxy with rate limiting rules — zero cost, no database needed.

### System Prompt (Draft)

This is the full system prompt for the MVP. It IS the product logic.

```
You are Revora, a food advisor built specifically for people with prediabetes (A1C 5.7–6.4).
Your job is to answer one question: "Can this person eat this food, given their A1C?"

The user will provide: (1) a food description, (2) their A1C reading.

Use the following A1C risk calibration:
- A1C 5.7–5.9: Mild prediabetes. More flexibility. Reserve HIGH classification for
  clearly high-glycemic foods (white rice, white bread, sugary drinks, baked goods).
- A1C 6.0–6.2: Moderate prediabetes. Apply standard thresholds. Most mixed meals will
  be MODERATE. Clearly safe foods (non-starchy vegetables, lean proteins) are SAFE.
- A1C 6.3–6.4: Upper-range prediabetes. Apply conservative thresholds. When uncertain,
  classify as MODERATE rather than SAFE. Recommend sequencing for any carb-containing meal.

Output format — always use this exact structure:

**[SAFE / MODERATE / HIGH RISK]** — [one sentence plain-English reason]

[If SAFE]: Great news — [specific reassurance about why this food is fine]. [Optional:
one simple tip if it would help]. You can eat this.

[If MODERATE or HIGH]:
To reduce the blood sugar impact: [one sequencing instruction using vegetables → protein
→ carbs order, OR a specific eating-speed tip]. This is based on research showing that
eating order can reduce blood sugar spikes by up to 30%.

Consider swapping [current item] for [specific alternative] — it has a lower glycemic
impact for people with prediabetes and [one taste/practicality note].

---
*Informational guidance only — not medical advice. Always consult your doctor or a
registered dietitian for personalized medical guidance.*

Edge cases:
- If the input is not a food item: Respond "That doesn't look like a food description.
  Try entering something like 'white rice with chicken' or 'a banana.'"
- If the food is ambiguous (e.g., "the thing at Chipotle"): Ask one clarifying question:
  "Which item? For example, burrito bowl, tacos, or something else?"
- If the meal is carbs-only with no protein/vegetable (e.g., "plain white rice", "pasta"):
  Skip the sequencing instruction. Instead: "For a carbs-only meal, consider adding a
  protein (chicken, eggs, tofu) or vegetables to the same plate — this significantly
  reduces the blood sugar impact."
- If A1C is not in the 5.7–6.4 range: If below 5.7, note "Your A1C is below the prediabetes
  range — this tool is designed for people with A1C 5.7–6.4. The guidance may still be
  useful, but see your doctor for personalized advice." If 6.5 or above, note "Your A1C
  is in the Type 2 diabetes range — Revora is designed for prediabetes. Please work
  with your doctor or a certified diabetes educator for appropriate guidance."
- For swaps: Never suggest swaps for SAFE-classified foods. The job is permission, not
  restriction.
- Tone rule: For SAFE results, lead with good news and be warm. For MODERATE/HIGH,
  be direct but not alarming. Never use "dangerous [REVIEW NEEDED: Replace restriction-framing with permission-first language]", "harmful", "bad for you." Use
  "higher blood sugar impact" instead.
- GL quantification: Do NOT state specific GL numbers. GPT-4o does not have a validated
  internal GL database. Use qualitative language only: "lower glycemic impact",
  "higher blood sugar impact", "significantly reduces the spike."
```

### Mobile UX Requirements

The user will be standing in a kitchen, restaurant, or grocery store when they use this.
- Input field must work with mobile virtual keyboard (no multi-line text area for A1C)
- Two inputs: food description (text field, no auto-focus — auto-focus triggers the keyboard
  immediately on mobile and obscures the screen), A1C (numeric input, 1 decimal place)
- Single CTA button: "Should I eat this?" — large, thumb-reachable at bottom of screen
- **Loading state**: Show a loading indicator on the submit button during the API call
  (e.g., "Checking..." with a spinner). Target response time is under 3 seconds; acceptable
  ceiling is 5 seconds. If the call exceeds 5 seconds, show: "Still checking — GPT-4o can
  be slow on busy networks."
- **Error state**: If the API call fails (timeout, 500, rate limit hit), show: "Couldn't check
  this food — try again in a moment. If it keeps failing, search r/prediabetes for this food
  and you'll find community answers." Never show a blank screen or a raw error message.
- Result text: minimum 16px font, high contrast, readable in bright light
- No modal, no navigation, no headers — single-screen, single-action

## Open Questions

1. **Naming collision**: "glocusnap" is very close to "Glycemic Snap" (glycemicsnap.com).
   App Store search, word-of-mouth confusion, and potential trademark issues need
   investigation before launch. The MVP web app doesn't need App Store approval, but if
   the brand lands, the collision needs to be resolved before the full product ships. The name changed to Revora.

2. **The permission framing in the full product**: The PRD UX uses SAFE/MODERATE/HIGH as
   traffic-light framing. The office hours session identified that SAFE results should feel
   like gifts, not passing grades. When designing the full product UI, the SAFE result
   screen needs explicit design work to feel enabling rather than clinical.

3. **A1C privacy**: The MVP sends A1C values to the OpenAI API. OpenAI's enterprise
   zero-data-retention policy applies to API calls only if explicitly configured with a
   ZDR agreement. Investigate OpenAI's current data retention defaults before launch.
   For the MVP this is low risk (no storage, no account) but worth understanding.

4. **WTP validation pricing**: The assignment uses $5/month as the probe price. The target
   product price is $13/month. These are not contradictory — the probe tests willingness
   to pay *anything*, not willingness to pay the final price. If people pay $5 easily,
   the $13 target becomes more defensible. Document this explicitly when reporting results.

## Amendment 1 — A1C Reversal Roadmap (Full Product Scope)

*This amendment supersedes PRD §6.4 Component B and the A1C estimation algorithm in the
Technical Specification. It does not affect the Permission MVP (which has no A1C tracking).*

### The Problem with the Original Formula

The PRD §6.4 Component B contains a Rust function that estimates A1C from GL adherence:
daily_change based on adherence ratio → projected A1C over 14-day window. This formula
has no supporting citation in peer-reviewed literature. It is a fabricated clinical
relationship between a proxy metric (GL adherence) and a lab value (A1C).

Users who follow the app faithfully but whose A1C doesn't improve will experience betrayal
and churn. Any dietitian, journalist, or App Store reviewer who investigates this claim can
collapse product credibility in one sentence.

### Replacement: Behavioral Adherence Index (BAI)

**Design principle**: Show the behavior that the evidence says drives reversal. Never claim to predict a specific lab value.

The BAI is a 0–100 composite score computed weekly:

| Dimension | Weight | Measurement |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| GL Budget Adherence | 50% | Days within daily GL budget / 7 |
| Scan Consistency | 30% | Meals scanned / estimated meals eaten (target: 3/day) |
| Post-Meal Action | 20% | Post-meal walk reminders completed / set |

| BAI | Label | Contextual Message |
|-----|-------|--------------------|
| 80–100 | Excellent | "Your consistency this week matches participants who reversed prediabetes in the CDC DPP trial." |
| 60–79 | On Track | "Most weeks at this level are associated with meaningful A1C improvement over 90 days." |
| 40–59 | Building | "You're establishing the habit. Consistency compounds — week 3 is typically harder than week 6." |
| 0–39 | Getting Started | "Every scan is a data point. You don't need a perfect week — you need a next meal." |

### Citation Foundation

All motivational copy referencing outcomes must cite one of:
- **CDC DPP**: 58% reduction in diabetes progression with diet + activity vs. placebo (NEJM, 2002)
- **Jenkins et al.**: Mean A1C reduction of 0.4 points over 24 weeks on GL dietary intervention (Am J Clin Nutr, 2008)
- **Imai et al.**: 29% reduction in postprandial glucose with vegetable-first eating order (Nutrients, 2023)

### Removed

**Remove entirely**: "Based on your last 14 days, you're on track to reach 5.8 by Day 90."
This is a medical prediction. It must not appear in any form in the product.

**Replace with**: "Based on your last 14 days of behavior, you're matching the consistency profile of participants who reduced A1C by 0.3–0.5 points in 90 days (CDC DPP)."

### Acceptance Criteria

- No screen in the app contains a predicted future A1C value
- BAI score is calculated server-side every Sunday at midnight
- BAI methodology is disclosed in the app's "How this works" section
- All clinical outcome references cite the CDC DPP or equivalent peer-reviewed source
