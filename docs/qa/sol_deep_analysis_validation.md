## Verdict on the original analysis

**Overall: substantially accurate and directionally sound.** Of 32 discrete findings validated: **23 CONFIRMED** (six of them positive controls verified working as designed), **7 PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, 1 NOT CONFIRMED** (the test-count figures), **1 not-validatable** (funnel estimates, correctly labeled hypotheses in the original). Within F-29's 19 external citations: 12 confirmed exactly, 5 partial, 2 wrong. The analysis's two largest calls — missing clinical-risk routing and the silent model downgrade — are both confirmed, and the downgrade is *worse* than described (it applies **only** to paying/trialing users; guests keep the better model). Two findings should be downgraded or corrected: the "reversal North Star" is a stale internal doc, not a shipped claim; and the cited test numbers (110 files / 86 safety / 19 smoke) are unsourced and wrong. Validation also surfaced **29 new findings** the analysis missed (see §New Findings) — the most serious being that pantry photos live in public-read blob storage and survive account deletion (contradicting the published privacy promise), that the safety-contract regexes are never enforced at runtime, and that the QA remediation record contains two claims contradicted by artifacts. On the positive side, adversarial review **confirmed no cross-account access path** (every data route filters on `session.userId`), consent enforced server-side, a correctly built reviewer backdoor, and a prod-locked video-engine spawn surface.

## Bottom line

The original analysis is validated as **directionally correct with two overreaches and one under-reach**: it correctly identified the two most serious product risks (clinical scope-blindness, paid-model downgrade — the latter proving worse than claimed), overstated the reversal-claim and popularity-badge exposure, and missed an entire class of enforcement-gap findings (claims regexes unenforced at runtime, mock-based evals, inactive CI, missing feedback instrumentation) that this validation adds.

Under the severity rubric used here, Revora has **six open P0-class blockers** (F-09/F-10 clinical routing, F-21 paid-model downgrade, F-13 steady-choice, F-26 legal placeholders, F-06 validation gate, N-23 blob-deletion integrity) — so the honest verdict today is **NO-GO for a broad paid launch**, with a short, well-defined path back: four of the six are XS–M engineering fixes; the other two are external human processes (counsel, expert validation) that are already specified. See `revora_unconditional_go_implementation_plan.md` and `revora_release_scorecard.md`.
